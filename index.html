What Powers Our Voyage? Mantle Convection

Beneath Earth's solid crust lies the hot, semi-solid mantle. Deep inside the Earth, intense heat from the core warms the lower mantle rock, making it less dense so it rises toward the surface. As it nears the crust, it cools, becomes denser, and sinks back down. This continuous circular motion is called a convection current—the giant conveyor belt that pushes and pulls Earth's tectonic plates into each other and apart.

Passenger Field Guide: Essential Vocabulary

Convection

The circular movement of heated, less-dense mantle material rising and cooler, denser material sinking, which drives plate tectonics.

Divergent Boundary

A tectonic boundary where two plates move away from each other, allowing magma to rise and create new crust.

Convergent Boundary

A tectonic boundary where two plates collide, resulting in either subduction or mountain building.

Subduction Zone

A region at a convergent boundary where a denser oceanic plate sinks beneath a less-dense plate into the mantle.
